## Interpretation

1. **Deployable routers are FVD-neutral.** All four land within ±1.5 of NO-TTA
   (80.7–82.7) and beat the fixed single config (84.8). Routing does not push
   the generated distribution away from GT — it modestly helps vs committing to
   one config.
2. **Routers capture ≈none of the oracle's FVD headroom.** Oracle reaches 72.3
   (−8.9 vs NO-TTA); the best feature-based router reaches 80.7 (−0.5),
   ≈6% of the gap. Consistent with the PSNR/VBench routability diagnostics
   (R²(gain) ≤ 0): features cannot predict the per-video winning config.
3. **Skip (13-action) helps FVD** over always-adapting (12-action) for both
   metrics — when unsure, picking NO-TTA (~58% of videos) keeps FVD near
   NO-TTA's 81 rather than the fixed config's 84.8.
4. **PSNR-motivated routers > VBench-motivated on FVD**, as expected: FVD is a
   fidelity/distribution metric, closer in spirit to PSNR than to VBench's
   semantic dimensions.

## Takeaway for the paper

The FVD story mirrors the PSNR/VBench story: **real oracle headroom exists
(−8.9 FVD, −0.38 dB PSNR), but it is not routable from cheap per-video
features.** Deployable routing is safe (FVD-neutral, never worse than NO-TTA
when skip is allowed) but does not deliver the oracle's gains.
