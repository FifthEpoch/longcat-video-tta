# New hypotheses on per-video TTA suitability — research synthesis 2026-06-09

**Author:** AdaSteer paper preparation, literature pass for next experimental wave
**Companion to:** [REVIEW_per_video_tta_suitability_2026-06-09.md](REVIEW_per_video_tta_suitability_2026-06-09.md)
**Status:** Cluster is in maintenance; this document scopes new feature batteries for the next experimental wave.

## TL;DR

At Panda-70M 1000-video / 480p / 17-frame standard horizon, every obvious-axis predictor of ΔPSNR (RAFT mean optical-flow, caption length, baseline PSNR) is null (|Spearman ρ| ≤ 0.09 across all 6 TTA methods). The literature points at three richer mechanisms we have not yet measured: **(1) "OOD relative to the diffusion model" via diffusion-loss / score-norm geometry (Heng et al. NeurIPS 2024 DiffPath; Barkley et al. 2025 SCOPED)** — the parallel-workstream caption-conditioned diffusion-loss-as-OOD experiment is a direct mechanism test of this; **(2) per-sample influence / single-step over-fit leverage (Mlodozeniec et al. ICLR 2025; Kwon et al. ICLR 2024 DataInf; Garg & Roy CVPR 2023)** — predicts that the panda_0098 catastrophe (44.55→22.16 dB under LoRA-r8) is a DreamBooth-style one-shot over-fit, detectable cheaply via a one-Adam-step in-loop loss drop; **(3) frequency-domain / intrinsic-dimension complexity beyond optical-flow (Dieleman 2024; Kamkari et al. NeurIPS 2024 FLIPD)** — high-frequency-band energy ratio and local intrinsic dimension are predicted to gate the LoRA-only ΔPSNR tail. We propose 12 hypotheses spanning themes A/B/C/D/E/G, of which **6 are Tier-1 and runnable now on a single GPU**, **5 are Tier-2 and bolt onto the parallel diffusion-OOD experiment's forward-pass infrastructure at near-zero marginal cost**, and **2 are Tier-3 mini-TTA-style probes** that we expect to use as confirmatory rather than exploratory tools.

The single most actionable finding from the literature is a **caveat for the parallel diffusion-OOD experiment**: Serrà et al. (ICLR 2020) showed that generative-model likelihoods on natural images are dominated by lossless-compression bits-per-pixel — i.e. by raw input complexity, not semantic OOD-ness. The diffusion-loss-as-OOD score is therefore confounded with H.264-bpp unless the bpp covariate is regressed out. H-T1-2 below specifies how to do that without re-running the OOD experiment.

## 1. Why we need new hypotheses

The 2026-06-09 per-video gain analysis (`sweep_experiment/reports/per_video_analysis/2026-06-09/summary.md`) intersected 999 videos across NOTTA + 6 TTA methods (ADA, ADA_NOPROMPT, LORA_R8_TTA, LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT, TL_BARE_R2, TL_TIED_R2). On every method, all three obvious-axis correlations are inside ±0.09 Spearman:

- ρ(ΔPSNR, RAFT mean-flow): max |ρ| = 0.088 (`ADA_NOPROMPT`)
- ρ(ΔPSNR, baseline PSNR): max |ρ| = 0.088 (`LORA_R8_TTA`)
- ρ(ΔPSNR, caption word count): max |ρ| = 0.062 (`TL_TIED_R2`)

…even though the per-method tails are non-degenerate (e.g. LORA_R8_TTA has 7 videos with ΔPSNR > +1.0 dB and 21 with ΔPSNR < -1.0 dB, plus one catastrophic outlier — panda_0098, 44.55→22.16 dB). The ΔPSNR signal is real per-video; the explanatory axes are wrong. The TTA-suitability literature has examined a much richer feature space than this. The hypotheses below come from a directed pass over six literature themes (per-sample TTA selection; OOD detection for diffusion / generative models; influence functions and loss-landscape curvature; video-specific complexity beyond optical-flow; caption-video alignment quality; asymmetric / pathological-failure prediction), and the existing Tier-1 feature extractor at `scripts/extract_video_features_for_tta.py` is used as the canonical "what already exists" reference.

## 2. Hypotheses (organised by tier)

Each hypothesis below is **predictive** (states the sign of the correlation under the hypothesis), **falsifiable** (states what observed result would refute it), and **non-redundant** with the existing extractor's columns (mean-flow / caption-length / baseline-PSNR are excluded).

### Tier-1 — cheap, runnable now (single GPU, < 5 s/video on H200)

#### H-T1-1: VAE-reconstruction error in the LongCat encoder–decoder round-trip predicts |ΔPSNR| asymmetrically

- **Statement:** Videos with higher LongCat-VAE round-trip reconstruction error on the TTA-visible 48-frame window will have larger **|ΔPSNR|** under every TTA method (both bigger wins and bigger losses), and a larger **ΔPSNR** under LoRA-class methods specifically.
- **Mechanism:** LongCat-Video is a latent-space diffusion transformer. Two recent results (Ding et al. 2025 *Revisiting Likelihood-Based OOD by Modeling Representations*; Järve et al. 2025 *Probability Density from Latent Diffusion Models for OOD*) demonstrate that diffusion-model likelihoods recover their textbook OOD-detection behaviour when computed in encoder-latent rather than pixel space — and conversely that pixel-space mismatch (visible as elevated VAE round-trip error) is exactly the signal that a video's latents lie in a non-typical region of the encoder's distribution. The TTL-LLM result (arXiv:2505.20633) further shows that in *generative* TTA (unlike classifier TTA), high-perplexity (i.e. atypical) samples gain more from a test-time update.
- **Measurable feature:** `rec_err_l1 = mean_{t,c,h,w} | x_thw − Decoder(Encoder(x))_thw |` on the TTA-visible 48 frames; add `rec_err_lpips` per frame, averaged.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (one VAE forward + one VAE backward-decode per video; the LongCat VAE is much cheaper than the diffusion transformer).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented anywhere. Closest existing infrastructure: the runners (`delta_experiment/scripts/common.py`) already wrap `VAE.encode` and `VAE.decode`.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ with |ΔPSNR| on all 6 methods; positive ρ with ΔPSNR on the four LoRA-class methods (LORA_R8_TTA{,_NOPROMPT}, TL_BARE_R2, TL_TIED_R2).
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 with |ΔPSNR| on all 6 methods *and* the signed-ΔPSNR LoRA-class effect is also < 0.10. That would rule out latent-space typicality as the operative axis at this horizon.
- **Citation:** [Ding et al. 2025, *Revisiting Likelihood-Based OOD by Modeling Representations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07793); [Järve et al. 2025, *Probability Density from Latent Diffusion Models for OOD*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15737).

#### H-T1-2: Lossless-compression bits-per-pixel is a confound for the parallel diffusion-OOD experiment (and an orthogonal complexity signal in its own right)

- **Statement:** Per-video H.264 bits-per-pixel (`bpp`) has a positive Spearman ρ ≥ 0.4 with the parallel-workstream diffusion-loss-as-OOD score; after regressing `bpp` out, the *residual* diffusion-OOD-vs-ΔPSNR correlation will be smaller but non-zero; and `bpp` alone has |ρ| ≤ 0.15 with ΔPSNR (because complexity-of-input does not by itself drive TTA gain — it drives only the *pixel-space* generative likelihood bias).
- **Mechanism:** Serrà et al. (ICLR 2020, arXiv:1909.11480) prove that pixel-space generative-model log-likelihoods are dominated by input complexity, quantified as lossless-compression length. A diffusion-loss-based OOD score is *also* a pixel-space (or VAE-latent-space) generative-likelihood score. Without subtracting the compression-length covariate, the parallel-OOD experiment is at risk of measuring "how complex is the input" rather than "how unfamiliar is the input". The corrected likelihood-ratio score (negative-log-likelihood minus compression-length) is the actual Theme-B OOD signal.
- **Measurable feature:** `bpp_h264 = file_size_bytes × 8 / (T × H × W)` from the original Panda mp4; complementary `bpp_png_avg = (1/T) Σ_t PNG_size(frame_t) × 8 / (H × W)` to disentangle inter-frame redundancy from intra-frame complexity.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (one `ffprobe` + at most one `cv2.imencode` per frame; < 0.5 s/video).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented anywhere. **This must be added before the parallel diffusion-OOD experiment is interpreted.**
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ ≥ 0.4 with the parallel diffusion-OOD score; |ρ| ≤ 0.15 with ΔPSNR on its own; non-zero residual ΔPSNR-vs-(OOD − α·bpp) ρ after partial-out.
- **Falsification criterion:** ρ(bpp, diffusion-OOD-score) < 0.3 across the 1000-video set — that would refute the Serrà et al. confound *in this VAE-latent setting* and free the parallel-OOD experiment from needing the covariate adjustment.
- **Citation:** [Serrà et al. 2020, *Input Complexity and Out-of-distribution Detection with Likelihood-based Generative Models*, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11480).

#### H-T1-3: 3D-spatiotemporal high-frequency-band FFT energy ratio predicts LoRA-class ΔPSNR

- **Statement:** Videos with a higher fraction of spectral energy in the upper half of the 3D-spatiotemporal Fourier domain have larger ΔPSNR under the four LoRA-class TTA methods specifically; AdaSteer (no added capacity) shows a near-zero correlation.
- **Mechanism:** Dieleman (2024) and the Spectral Progressive Diffusion line of work (Yu et al. 2025, arXiv:2605.18736) establish that diffusion models implement approximate spectral autoregression: low-frequency content is generated early in the denoising trajectory, high-frequency emerges late. A pretrained model under-fits high frequencies more than low ones because the per-pixel ε-loss budget is dominated by low-frequency content. LoRA TTA has spare rank capacity to target the under-fit high-frequency band; AdaSteer's δ-tuning of attention adapters has no such spare capacity and is predicted to be insensitive to this axis. This is also the axis-of-difficulty implicit in FADE (CVPR 2025) and the FFT-spatial vs FFT-spatiotemporal question for the open-questions section.
- **Measurable feature:** `hf_energy_ratio = ( Σ_{ω : |ω| > 0.5·Nyquist} |F(x)|² ) / ( Σ_ω |F(x)|² )` for a 3D (T×H×W) real FFT of the luma channel of the TTA-visible window; optionally per-channel as well.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (single 3D FFT per video, ~100 ms on CPU for 48×480×832).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ with ΔPSNR for LORA_R8_TTA{,_NOPROMPT}, TL_BARE_R2, TL_TIED_R2; near-zero ρ for ADA and ADA_NOPROMPT.
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 on all four LoRA-class methods.
- **Citation:** [Dieleman 2024, *Diffusion is Spectral Autoregression*](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html); [Yu et al. 2025, *Spectral Progressive Diffusion*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18736); [Zhu et al. CVPR 2025, *FADE: Frequency-Aware Diffusion Model Factorization for Video Editing*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Zhu_FADE_Frequency-Aware_Diffusion_Model_Factorization_for_Video_Editing_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf).

#### H-T1-4: RAFT optical-flow distribution shape (max, entropy, max/mean ratio) — not the mean — predicts ΔPSNR

- **Statement:** The *distributional shape* of per-pixel RAFT optical-flow magnitude — specifically `flow_max`, `flow_entropy = H(softmax(|flow|))`, and `flow_max / flow_mean` — has |ρ| > 0.15 with ΔPSNR on at least one TTA method despite mean-flow being null at |ρ| ≤ 0.09. The diagnostic prediction is that videos with high max-flow but low mean-flow (localised fast motion against a static background) gain more under AdaSteer than the population baseline; videos with uniformly-high mean-flow (a fast camera pan with no salient subject) are TTA-neutral.
- **Mechanism:** Mean-flow integrates a per-frame, per-pixel quantity twice — across pixels and across frames — and discards the *distribution* of difficulty within the clip. A SAR-style "reliable sample identification" argument (Niu et al. ICLR 2023): if the TTA loss has sparse high-loss regions (a handful of hard pixels per frame), the gradient is dominated by those pixels and the rest of the clip is wasted compute. Localised fast motion is exactly that regime; uniform fast motion is not. This generalises the existing null-mean-flow finding into a per-clip *concentration* axis.
- **Measurable feature:** `flow_max = max_{t, h, w} ||RAFT(x_t, x_{t+1})_{h, w}||_2`; `flow_entropy = -Σ_i p_i log p_i` where `p_i = softmax(|flow_i|.flatten())`; `flow_max_over_mean = flow_max / mean_flow_existing_column`.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (RAFT is already deployed for the `dynamic_degree.json` mean-flow pipeline; computing max/entropy/ratio at extraction time is free).
- **Implementation status:** Mean-flow is already extracted; max-flow, entropy, and the ratio are not.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ between `flow_max_over_mean` and ΔPSNR for ADA / ADA_NOPROMPT; near-zero for the LoRA-class methods (which use a different optimisation regime).
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 for all three shape statistics across all six methods.
- **Citation:** [Teed & Deng 2020, *RAFT: Recurrent All-Pairs Field Transforms for Optical Flow*, ECCV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12039); [Niu et al. 2023, *Towards Stable Test-time Adaptation in Dynamic Wild World*, ICLR (SAR)](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=g2YraF75Tj).

#### H-T1-5: Per-frame minimum CLIP-text-image similarity — not the mean — predicts ΔPSNR for caption-using methods

- **Statement:** The *minimum* per-frame CLIP-text-image cosine similarity (over the 48 TTA-visible frames) has |ρ| > 0.15 with ΔPSNR on the three caption-using TTA methods (ADA, LORA_R8_TTA, TL_BARE_R2 — TL_TIED_R2 is also caption-conditioned), and a substantially smaller |ρ| on the NOPROMPT variants (so the gap between caption-using and NOPROMPT ρ is itself > 0.05).
- **Mechanism:** ImageReward (Xu et al. NeurIPS 2023) showed that CLIPScore averaged over an image set has much weaker per-sample discriminability than alternative per-sample reward models; but the *minimum* over a temporal sequence captures "weakest-link" caption violations — a single frame where the caption visually fails to match the content. Under caption-conditioned TTA, the noised-target loss at that worst-fitting frame produces the largest caption-faithful gradient and dominates the held-out generation prediction. This complements caption length being null: long captions can still be locally vague, and short captions can still be locally precise; the min-frame fidelity is the operative axis.
- **Measurable feature:** `clip_text_image_sim_min` — **already extracted in `scripts/extract_video_features_for_tta.py:760`**. The correlation has simply not yet been computed against ΔPSNR.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (zero marginal — feature is already extracted).
- **Implementation status:** **Feature already extracted; needs only a correlation pass through `scripts/correlate_tta_gain_with_features.py`.**
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ ≥ +0.15 with ΔPSNR on ADA, LORA_R8_TTA, TL_BARE_R2, TL_TIED_R2; near-zero ρ on ADA_NOPROMPT, LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT; and the prompt-vs-NOPROMPT ρ gap should itself be > 0.05.
- **Falsification criterion:** Both the absolute ρ < 0.15 *and* the prompt-vs-NOPROMPT gap < 0.05 across all four caption-using methods.
- **Citation:** [Xu et al. NeurIPS 2023, *ImageReward: Learning and Evaluating Human Preferences for Text-to-Image Generation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05977); [Hessel et al. 2021, *CLIPScore: A Reference-free Evaluation Metric*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08718).

#### H-T1-6: PySceneDetect hidden-cut count predicts the LoRA-r8 ΔPSNR catastrophic tail

- **Statement:** Among the 21 videos with `LORA_R8_TTA` ΔPSNR < -1.0 dB, a disproportionate fraction (≥ 25%, vs. ~7% baseline rate) will have ≥ 1 PySceneDetect-ContentDetector cut inside the TTA-visible window. The mechanism is that a scene-cut inside the TTA-context split (frames 0..13 clean context vs 14..47 noised target) creates a non-stationary loss landscape that drives the LoRA optimiser off-manifold.
- **Mechanism:** SAR (Niu et al. ICLR 2023) and SoTTA (Gong et al. NeurIPS 2023) show that loss-landscape sharpness on the per-sample objective is the precondition for TTA-driven model collapse. A scene-cut between the 14-frame context and the 34-frame noised target introduces a *categorical* non-stationarity: the temporal-attention layers see context tokens drawn from a different scene than the target tokens. The per-step gradient direction estimated on the cross-cut window is mis-aligned with the held-out generation window. The existing extractor's `cut_count_pyscenedetect` and `cut_count_histogram` columns are already populated; we just have not yet asked the asymmetric-tails question of them.
- **Measurable feature:** `cut_count_pyscenedetect` (already in `scripts/extract_video_features_for_tta.py`) restricted to the TTA-visible window; supplemented by `cut_count_histogram` (Bhattacharyya threshold = 0.40) as a backup.
- **Cost:** Tier-1 (zero marginal — feature is already extracted).
- **Implementation status:** **Feature already extracted; needs only an asymmetric-tail conditional analysis** (Fisher exact test on "ΔPSNR < -1.0 dB" × "≥ 1 cut").
- **Expected ρ sign:** Fisher exact OR ≥ 3.0 on `LORA_R8_TTA` and `LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT`; OR ≈ 1.0 on ADA / ADA_NOPROMPT (which have internal regularisation that should be more cut-robust); TinyLoRA-r2 in between.
- **Falsification criterion:** Fisher OR ≤ 1.5 on both LoRA-r8 methods.
- **Citation:** [Niu et al. ICLR 2023, SAR](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=g2YraF75Tj); [Gong et al. NeurIPS 2023, SoTTA](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/2da53cd1abdae59150e35f4693834f32-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Tier-2 — single-forward-pass through LongCat-Video (~30 s/video on H200)

#### H-T2-1: Caption-conditioned diffusion ε-loss as OOD-score — **parallel workstream, already being implemented**

- **Statement:** Per-video mean caption-conditioned diffusion loss `L_ε(x, c) = E_{t, ε} ||ε - ε_θ(x_t, c, t)||²` evaluated at the TTA-visible 48-frame latents, averaged over 3–5 noise levels t and 4–8 noise samples, has positive Spearman ρ with ΔPSNR on the LoRA-class TTA methods, after partialling out `bpp_h264` (H-T1-2) as a covariate.
- **Mechanism:** Direct application of the AnoDDPM / DDPM-OOD reconstruction-error family (Graham et al. CVPRW 2023; Pinaya et al. 2022) to a latent video diffusion model: a sample's expected denoising error is its negative-log-density up to a constant. Caption-conditioned loss is the natural Theme-B OOD-relative-to-the-conditional-diffusion-model score. **Threat from the literature:** Serrà et al. (ICLR 2020) — pixel-space generative likelihoods are confounded by lossless-compression bpp; the same confound likely applies in VAE-latent space because the LongCat VAE is itself a learned compressor.
- **Measurable feature:** parallel-workstream definition.
- **Cost:** Tier-2.
- **Implementation status:** **Being implemented in the parallel sibling workstream.** This document does not re-spec it; see that workstream's design doc for noise-level schedule, batch size, and stochastic-vs-deterministic timestep sampling decisions.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ with ΔPSNR on LoRA-class methods; ambiguous on AdaSteer.
- **Falsification criterion:** see the parallel workstream's protocol; this document only adds the bpp-partial-out requirement (H-T1-2).
- **Citation:** [Graham et al. CVPRW 2023, *Denoising Diffusion Models for Out-of-Distribution Detection*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07740); [Pinaya et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13726).

#### H-T2-2: SCOPED score-norm at a fixed mid-noise level

- **Statement:** The squared norm of the model's denoising prediction `||ε_θ(x_t*, c, t*)||²` at a fixed mid-noise level t* (e.g. t* ∈ {200, 500, 800} on a 1000-step schedule), averaged over 4–8 noise samples, has positive ρ with ΔPSNR on LoRA-class methods. This is **distinct** from the parallel-workstream diffusion-loss-as-OOD score (which measures ε-vs-target *mismatch*); the score-norm measures the *magnitude* of the score field at the input — the local "energy" of the data-density gradient — which is the dominant SCOPED component.
- **Mechanism:** SCOPED (Barkley et al. arXiv:2510.01456) shows that a one-forward-pass single-Hutchinson-JVP score-norm approximation is competitive with multi-pass diffusion-OOD detectors. The relationship to TTA suitability: a large score norm at the unadapted weights indicates the diffusion model is locally "pushing hard" at the input, which under a small parameter perturbation (LoRA TTA) is exactly the regime where the largest swing in p_θ(x) per unit of optimisation budget is possible.
- **Measurable feature:** `score_norm_t* = E_ε || ε_θ(x_t*, c, t*) ||² / (T·C·H·W)` for t* ∈ {200, 500, 800}; reuse the same noise-sample batch as H-T2-1 to amortise cost (negligible marginal).
- **Cost:** Tier-2; **bolts onto the parallel-OOD forward-pass batch at near-zero marginal cost** (same forward, just record `||ε||²` instead of `||ε - target||²`).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented; can be added to the parallel-workstream's eval script as a single extra line per forward pass.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ with ΔPSNR on LoRA-class methods; near-zero on AdaSteer.
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 across all four LoRA-class methods, AND the parallel-OOD diffusion-loss score has |ρ| > 0.15. That would mean loss-mismatch is the operative axis and score-norm-geometry is not.
- **Citation:** [Barkley et al. 2025, *SCOPED: Score–Curvature Out-of-distribution Proximity Evaluator for Diffusion*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01456); [Heng et al. NeurIPS 2024, *DiffPath*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11881).

#### H-T2-3: Per-sample classifier-free-guidance gap predicts the caption-using-vs-NOPROMPT split

- **Statement:** Per-video mean CFG gap `||ε_θ(x_t, c, t) - ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t)|| / ||ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t)||` (averaged over t, frames, and noise samples) has positive ρ ≥ +0.15 with ΔPSNR on the three caption-using methods (ADA, LORA_R8_TTA, the two TL methods are also caption-conditioned), near-zero ρ on the two NOPROMPT variants, and the prompt-using-vs-NOPROMPT ρ gap is itself > 0.10.
- **Mechanism:** The CFG gap measures how much information the caption actually carries about the score field at each (x, t). Recent work on dynamic CFG (Imagen 3 dynamic CFG, ICLR 2026 OpenReview z9YC9bvfUL; CFG stage-wise dynamics, ICLR 2026 OpenReview fP0s1TEow3) demonstrates that the optimal CFG scale is strongly per-sample and per-stage — i.e., per-sample CFG gap varies meaningfully across a 1000-video set. A low-CFG-gap video is one for which the caption is essentially generic relative to the score field, so caption-conditioned TTA degenerates to caption-dropped TTA — explaining how caption length can be null while CFG-gap is non-null. This is a direct mechanism check for whether the caption-conditioned TTA loss is operationally caption-aware on each video.
- **Measurable feature:** `cfg_gap = E_{t, ε, frame} ||ε_θ(x_t, c, t) - ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t)||_2 / max(||ε_θ(x_t, ∅, t)||_2, 1e-6)` at the same noise samples as H-T2-1 / H-T2-2.
- **Cost:** Tier-2 (factor of 2 over H-T2-2: needs both conditional and unconditional ε per noise sample; same forward-pass schedule and noise pre-computation).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented; bolts onto the parallel-OOD eval script with one extra unconditional ε call per noise sample.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ ≥ +0.15 with ΔPSNR on the four caption-using methods; near-zero ρ on the two NOPROMPT methods; the prompt-vs-NOPROMPT gap is > 0.10.
- **Falsification criterion:** Both the absolute ρ < 0.15 *and* the prompt-vs-NOPROMPT gap < 0.05.
- **Citation:** [Jin et al. ICLR 2026, *Stage-wise Dynamics of Classifier-Free Guidance*](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fP0s1TEow3); [Imagen team 2026 (under review), *Dynamic Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance via Online Feedback*](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z9YC9bvfUL).

#### H-T2-4: FLIPD local intrinsic dimension predicts LoRA-class ΔPSNR

- **Statement:** The FLIPD estimator (Kamkari et al. NeurIPS 2024) of local intrinsic dimension at the video's VAE-latent, evaluated using the LongCat ε-network at a small noise level, has positive ρ with ΔPSNR on LoRA-class methods; near-zero on AdaSteer.
- **Mechanism:** FLIPD reads off LID from the Fokker-Planck equation associated with a pretrained diffusion model — i.e. directly from a forward pass and a Hutchinson Hessian-trace estimate. LID quantifies how many local factors of variation the manifold has at a given point. High-LID neighbourhoods are where the pretrained model has spread its capacity thinnest; LoRA's low-rank addition concentrates extra capacity precisely along the locally-dominant directions of variation. AdaSteer adds no parameters and is predicted to be insensitive to LID.
- **Measurable feature:** `lid_flipd` per Equation 7 of [arXiv:2406.03537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03537), evaluated at small t (e.g. equivalent to t = 50 on the LongCat schedule) using one forward pass + a Hutchinson estimate of `tr(∇²_x log p_t(x))`. Shares the H-T2-2 forward pass.
- **Cost:** Tier-2; bolts onto the parallel-OOD batch.
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive for LoRA-class methods; near-zero for ADA.
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 across all four LoRA-class methods.
- **Citation:** [Kamkari et al. NeurIPS 2024, *A Geometric View of Data Complexity: Efficient Local Intrinsic Dimension Estimation with Diffusion Models* (FLIPD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03537).

#### H-T2-5: Diffusion-loss *variance* across noise levels — a generative analogue of EATA's entropy signal

- **Statement:** The variance of `L_ε(x, c; t)` across noise levels t ∈ {200, 500, 800} (i.e. how inconsistent the model is in its confidence at this video) has positive Spearman ρ ≥ +0.15 with ΔPSNR on the four LoRA-class methods. Low across-t variance means the model has a coherent view of this video (uniformly easy or uniformly hard); high across-t variance means there is a specific stage of the denoising trajectory where the model is uncertain — which is precisely where TTA's per-sample optimisation has its largest impact.
- **Mechanism:** Direct translation of EATA's entropy-based reliable-sample identification (Niu et al. ICML 2022) into the generative regime: entropy in a classifier ↔ across-t loss variance in a diffusion model. The TTL-LLM result (arXiv:2505.20633) further argues that in generative TTT, the high-perplexity (high-loss) samples are the ones that gain — but the *direction* of the gain depends on whether the loss is *consistently* high (sample is just plain hard — no signal to learn) or *intermittently* high (sample has a specific failure mode at one stage — signal exists). Across-t variance disambiguates these two cases.
- **Measurable feature:** `loss_var_t = Var_{t ∈ {t_low, t_mid, t_high}} E_{ε, frame} ||ε - ε_θ(x_t, c, t)||²`, reusing the parallel-OOD experiment's per-(t, noise-sample) loss values (this hypothesis literally just changes how those numbers are aggregated).
- **Cost:** Tier-2; bolts onto the parallel-OOD batch at *zero* extra forward passes if the parallel workstream stores per-t losses rather than only their mean.
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented; **specific request to the parallel-OOD workstream: store per-t losses, not just the mean across t.**
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ ≥ +0.15 with ΔPSNR on LoRA-class methods.
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 with ΔPSNR on all four LoRA-class methods, AND the mean-across-t parallel-OOD loss has |ρ| > 0.15 — meaning the absolute loss level dominates over the loss variance.
- **Citation:** [Niu et al. ICML 2022, *EATA*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/niu22a/niu22a.pdf); [Sun et al. 2025, *Test-Time Learning for Large Language Models* (TTL-LLM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20633).

### Tier-3 — expensive, multi-pass or mini-TTA (> 1 min/video on H200)

#### H-T3-1: Per-video gradient norm at the unadapted weights θ₀ — the SAR-style large-gradient predictor of asymmetric LoRA tails

- **Statement:** The L2 norm of the LoRA-tunable-parameter gradient of the TTA loss at the initial LongCat weights, `||∇_{θ_LoRA} L_diff(x_TTA, c; θ_0)||_2`, has |ρ| ≥ +0.15 with **|ΔPSNR|** under the LoRA-r8 methods (asymmetric-tail prediction) and negative ρ with **signed ΔPSNR** under LORA_R8_TTA / LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT specifically (because the catastrophic-tail mechanism — panda_0098 — is on this side).
- **Mechanism:** SAR (Niu et al. ICLR 2023) gives a precise classifier-TTA result: large per-sample gradient norms cause TTA-driven model collapse, and reliable-sample identification (filtering large-gradient samples) prevents the collapse. SLo-Curves (Garg & Roy CVPR 2023) shows the same quantity (Hessian-eigenvalue-based curvature, which gradient norm tracks at fixed step size) separates clean from atypical / occluded samples in classification. Translated to diffusion: a video whose ε-loss has large per-parameter gradient at θ_0 is one where a small LoRA step moves the model a long way locally — for an asymmetry-aware method (ADA's δ-tuning) this is rescue; for an unregularised low-rank method (LoRA-r8) this is over-fit. This is also DataInf's leverage-score quantity (Kwon et al. ICLR 2024) for the special case of a single-sample fine-tune of a LoRA adapter.
- **Measurable feature:** `grad_norm_θ0 = ||∇_{θ_LoRA} L_diff_TTA(x; θ_0)||_2` — one forward + one backward through the LoRA / AdaSteer parameter set at the unadapted weights, no optimiser step.
- **Cost:** Tier-3 (one forward + one backward per video per TTA method's parameter set; ≈ 1/N_TTA-steps of a full TTA run).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented; the closest existing scaffold is `delta_experiment/scripts/run_delta_a.py` and `lora_experiment/scripts/run_lora_tta.py`, both of which already build the full TTA optimiser — the new probe reads `optimizer.param_groups[0]['params'].grad` after a single backward.
- **Expected ρ sign:** positive ρ with |ΔPSNR| for LoRA-class methods (asymmetric-tail predictor); negative ρ with signed ΔPSNR for LORA_R8_TTA and LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT (the catastrophic-tail mechanism — high-grad videos overshoot); near-zero or positive signed ρ for ADA (which has internal δ-tuning regularisation).
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.15 with |ΔPSNR| on `LORA_R8_TTA` — that result would also rule out SAR's classifier→diffusion mapping for this setting.
- **Citation:** [Niu et al. ICLR 2023, *SAR*](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=g2YraF75Tj); [Garg & Roy CVPR 2023, *Samples with Low Loss Curvature Improve Data Efficiency*](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2023/poster/20980); [Kwon et al. ICLR 2024, *DataInf: Efficiently Estimating Data Influence in LoRA-tuned LLMs and Diffusion Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00902); [Mlodozeniec et al. ICLR 2025, *Influence Functions for Scalable Data Attribution in Diffusion Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13850).

#### H-T3-2: One-step LoRA-r8 in-loop loss drop — a DreamBooth-style overfit detector for the catastrophic-LoRA tail

- **Statement:** The fractional in-loop loss drop after a single Adam step on the LoRA-r8 adapter, `(L_diff(x; θ_0) − L_diff(x; θ_0 + step)) / L_diff(x; θ_0)`, has **negative** ρ ≤ -0.20 with `LORA_R8_TTA` ΔPSNR — the video that LoRA finds *easiest* to fit on the visible window is the video most likely to collapse on the held-out generation window. The panda_0098 catastrophe (44.55 → 22.16 dB) is predicted to lie in the top decile of one-step in-loop loss drop, despite its high baseline PSNR.
- **Mechanism:** DreamBooth (Ruiz et al. arXiv:2208.12242) documents the canonical failure of single-instance fine-tuning: without a prior-preservation loss, a small LoRA adapter trained on a single clip catastrophically forgets the class manifold and degenerates to a "render this exact clip" predictor. The single-step in-loop loss drop is the natural early-warning signal: a video that the LoRA adapter can fit in one step is precisely the kind of clip for which there is no prior-preservation regularisation pulling the optimiser away from the trivial "memorise the visible window" solution. Anti-CF (EMNLP 2023) and ZeroSiam (arXiv:2509.23183) extend the same argument to general TTA model-collapse. The asymmetric prediction is mechanistically distinct from H-T3-1: H-T3-1 says "high-curvature videos move a lot per step"; H-T3-2 says "videos whose in-loop loss drops quickly are over-fitting, regardless of curvature".
- **Measurable feature:** `single_step_loss_drop = (L_diff(x_TTA; θ_0) − L_diff(x_TTA; θ_0 + Adam_step)) / max(L_diff(x_TTA; θ_0), 1e-6)` — one forward + one backward + one Adam step + one forward pass.
- **Cost:** Tier-3 (≈ 2/N_TTA-steps of a full LoRA-r8 TTA run; cheaper than running TTA to completion).
- **Implementation status:** Not implemented. Reuses the LoRA TTA optimiser setup in `lora_experiment/scripts/run_lora_tta.py`.
- **Expected ρ sign:** Strongly negative ρ ≤ -0.20 with `LORA_R8_TTA` and `LORA_R8_TTA_NOPROMPT` ΔPSNR; near-zero or weakly negative with ADA (which has internal regularisation). The panda_0098 row should rank in the top-10% of `single_step_loss_drop` despite having 44.55 dB baseline PSNR.
- **Falsification criterion:** |ρ| ≤ 0.10 with `LORA_R8_TTA` ΔPSNR — that would refute the DreamBooth-collapse mechanism for LoRA-r8 TTA in this setting AND specifically rule out one-step loss drop as a screening signal for the catastrophic tail.
- **Citation:** [Ruiz et al. CVPR 2023, *DreamBooth*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242); [Ye et al. EMNLP 2023, *Anti-CF: Beware of Model Collapse*](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.803.pdf); [Liu et al. 2025, *ZeroSiam*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23183).

## 4. What the literature actually says

**Theme A — per-sample TTA selection.** The classifier-TTA literature converges on a low-entropy = reliable, high-entropy = unreliable / collapse-inducing selection criterion: EATA (Niu et al. ICML 2022, [PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/niu22a/niu22a.pdf)) shows that adapting on the low-entropy half of test samples *beats* adapting on the full set, because high-entropy samples produce noisy gradients. SAR (Niu et al. ICLR 2023, [PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=g2YraF75Tj)) formalises the same finding via gradient-norm: large-gradient samples drive model collapse, and entropy serves as a cheap thresholdable proxy because entropy and gradient-norm are tightly coupled. SoTTA (Gong et al. NeurIPS 2023, [PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/2da53cd1abdae59150e35f4693834f32-Paper-Conference.pdf)) extends this with sharpness-aware minimisation. **The most important caveat for our setting** comes from the generative side: TTL-LLM (arXiv:2505.20633) finds that in test-time *learning* for LLMs, **high-perplexity samples gain *more* — the opposite of the classifier finding** — because they carry the most new information for the model. Diffusion TTA is a generative regime; we should be open to either sign on the perplexity ↔ ΔPSNR axis, which is exactly what H-T2-5 (loss-variance) operationalises. TTT-MAE (Gandelsman et al. NeurIPS 2022, [PDF](https://yossigandelsman.github.io/ttt_mae/index.html)) showed that single-sample reconstruction loss is itself a viable TTA signal, which conceptually grounds H-T2-1 (loss-as-OOD) and H-T3-2 (in-loop loss drop).

**Theme B — OOD detection for diffusion / generative models.** Raw pixel-space generative-model likelihoods are famously broken for OOD detection: Nalisnick et al. (ICLR 2019) showed that flow models trained on CIFAR-10 assign higher likelihood to SVHN than to CIFAR-10. Two follow-ups explained the mechanism. **Typicality** (Nalisnick et al. 2019, [PDF](https://bayesiandeeplearning.org/2019/papers/22.pdf)): in-distribution samples lie in the *typical set* of the model, not in its mode; check membership in the typical set instead of comparing likelihoods. **Input complexity** (Serrà et al. ICLR 2020, [PDF](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.11480)): generative-model likelihoods on natural images are dominated by lossless-compression length; subtract compression-bpp from negative log-likelihood and OOD detection is rescued. **This is the most concrete threat to the parallel diffusion-OOD experiment**, formalised in H-T1-2. Modern dedicated diffusion-OOD methods sidestep raw likelihood: AnoDDPM / DDPM-OOD (Graham et al. CVPRW 2023, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07740)) uses reconstruction error at multiple noise levels; DiffPath (Heng et al. NeurIPS 2024, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11881)) uses rate-of-change and curvature of the diffusion trajectory; SCOPED (Barkley et al. 2025, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01456)) uses the score Jacobian-trace plus score-norm at one forward pass. The latest representation-space results (Ding et al. 2025 [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07793); Järve et al. 2025 [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15737)) show that likelihood-based OOD detection works *fine* if you do it in a learned encoder's latent space — directly relevant since LongCat-Video is itself a latent-space model. FLIPD (Kamkari et al. NeurIPS 2024, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03537)) extends the diffusion-as-density-model toolkit to local intrinsic dimension via the Fokker-Planck equation, with the bonus that LID correlates strongly with PNG compression rate — closing the loop with Theme D.

**Theme C — influence functions and loss landscapes.** Two recent results make per-sample influence tractable for our setting. Mlodozeniec et al. (ICLR 2025, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13850)) develop K-FAC GGN approximations of influence functions for diffusion models, with the explicit measurement function `m(θ, x) = log p_θ(x)`. DataInf (Kwon et al. ICLR 2024, [GitHub](https://github.com/ykwon0407/DataInf)) gives a closed-form, memory-efficient influence approximation specifically for LoRA-tuned LLMs and diffusion models — perfect for our LoRA-r8 setting. The diagnostic quantity we want is the *self-influence*: the per-sample gradient norm at the unadapted weights, which is the dominant term in DataInf's leverage score for a single-sample fine-tune (H-T3-1). On the curvature side, SLo-Curves (Garg & Roy CVPR 2023, [poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2023/poster/20980)) shows the empirical pattern: low-loss-curvature samples are clean and representative; high-curvature samples are occluded / atypical and dominate the data-efficient core-set selection. Per-sample loss curvature and per-sample gradient norm are tightly coupled, so SAR's reliable-sample story (Theme A) and SLo-Curves's curvature story (Theme C) collapse onto the same per-video predictor at fixed optimiser step size.

**Theme D — video-specific complexity beyond optical flow.** Three complementary toolkits. Information-theoretic: Normalised Shannon Entropy ([MDPI 27/2/166](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/27/2/166)) — already partly extracted as `rgb_histogram_entropy_mean`. ITU SI/TI and the VCA / IVCA pipeline (Menon et al. arXiv:2407.00280) use DCT-energy as a video-coding-complexity proxy. Bit-rate-based: lossless-compression bits-per-pixel (PNG, H.264) as a direct compressibility proxy — closing the loop with Serrà et al.'s OOD argument (H-T1-2). Diffusion-native: Dieleman's spectral-autoregression observation ([blog](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)) and the Spectral Progressive Diffusion paper (Yu et al. 2025, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18736)) motivate the FFT-high-frequency-ratio probe (H-T1-3): diffusion models are spectral autoregressors, so a video's high-frequency content is the most under-fit dimension and the natural target of LoRA TTA's extra capacity. FADE (Zhu et al. CVPR 2025, [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Zhu_FADE_Frequency-Aware_Diffusion_Model_Factorization_for_Video_Editing_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)) specifically uses 3D-spatiotemporal DFTs on diffusion-video latents and confirms that low-freq carries structure / motion, high-freq carries detail-and-noise. FLIPD (Kamkari et al. NeurIPS 2024) provides a diffusion-native LID estimator that is tractable at the scale of Stable Diffusion and tested at the scale of single-image FFHQ — the same forward-pass scale at which we operate.

**Theme E — caption-video alignment quality.** Two main findings shape the hypotheses. (1) **Per-sample CLIPScore has weak discriminability**: ImageReward (Xu et al. NeurIPS 2023, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05977)) directly shows that CLIPScore distributions per-sample have a much smaller interquartile range than ImageReward's, so CLIPScore *averages* miss per-sample structure that *order statistics* (the min-frame score, H-T1-5) recover. (2) **CFG is per-sample and per-stage**: dynamic-CFG papers (ICLR 2026 fP0s1TEow3 [link](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fP0s1TEow3); Imagen 3 dynamic CFG z9YC9bvfUL [link](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z9YC9bvfUL); adaptive-CFG SOC arXiv:2505.19367) demonstrate that the optimal guidance scale varies dramatically across prompts and across denoising stages — i.e., the *per-sample CFG gap* `||ε_cond − ε_uncond||` is a meaningful per-sample quantity. This is exactly what H-T2-3 measures. CLIPScore truncates to 77 tokens; for long captions a LongCLIP variant exists ([zer0int/LongCLIP-L-Diffusers](https://huggingface.co/zer0int/LongCLIP-L-Diffusers)) but at this horizon caption length is already null at |ρ| ≤ 0.06, so the truncation is not blocking.

**Theme F — TTA on video diffusion models.** Three 2025 results are directly relevant. TTT-Video (Xu et al. CVPR 2025, [GitHub](https://github.com/test-time-training/ttt-video-dit); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05298)) inserts TTT layers into CogVideoX-5B for one-minute generation; it does not study per-video TTA suitability but its architecture confirms that test-time training of *added* layers (analogous to our LoRA / TinyLoRA setting) is the dominant successful paradigm. Zo3T (Zhang et al. AAAI 2026, [GitHub](https://github.com/Richard-Zhang-AI/Zo3T-main); [arXiv:2509.06723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06723)) uses an *ephemeral* per-sample adapter for trajectory-guided I2V — making the connection between TTA and DreamBooth-style per-instance fine-tuning explicit. Video-T1 (Liu et al. arXiv:2503.18942) is test-time *scaling* rather than test-time training but its Tree-of-Frames result confirms that test-time compute spent on video diffusion has real per-prompt return. **None of these three papers report a per-sample TTA-suitability axis**, which is what makes this hypotheses document a contribution rather than a replication.

**Theme G — pathological / asymmetric failure prediction.** The clearest analogue to the panda_0098 catastrophe (44.55 → 22.16 dB under LoRA_R8_TTA) is the DreamBooth language-drift / class-manifold-forgetting failure mode (Ruiz et al. CVPR 2023, [arXiv:2208.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242); HuggingFace's [blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/dreambooth) explicitly warns that DreamBooth "overfits very quickly" and recommends low LRs + early stopping). The prior-preservation loss is exactly the regularisation that LoRA-r8 TTA *lacks* in our setting. Anti-CF (EMNLP 2023, [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.803.pdf)) generalises this to TTA in general: imbalanced label distributions cause Tent-style TTA to collapse all predictions to the majority class. ZeroSiam (arXiv:2509.23183) and ASR (Adaptive Selective Reset, arXiv:2603.03796) propose architectural-asymmetry and conditional-reset solutions; neither is directly relevant to single-sample diffusion TTA but their *signal-of-impending-collapse* features — a too-fast in-loop loss drop, logit-norm inflation, prediction-distribution skew — are the basis for H-T3-2 (one-step loss drop screener).

## 6. Open questions for the lead author

1. **Diffusion-OOD score caption-conditioning.** Should the parallel-workstream diffusion-loss-OOD score be evaluated caption-conditioned, unconditional, or both? The Theme-B literature is split: Heng et al. (DiffPath) and Barkley et al. (SCOPED) both work *unconditionally* (the score field's *geometry* doesn't depend on conditioning); but the AnoDDPM family computes reconstruction error against the noise that *was added*, which is conditioning-independent only when the model itself is unconditional. For our setting where the score-norm SCOPED proxy is conditional (H-T2-2) and CFG-gap explicitly compares conditional vs unconditional (H-T2-3), the cleanest scientific choice is **both**: store conditional, unconditional, and their gap, all at the same noise samples. Cost is ≤ 2× the conditional-only run.

2. **FFT spatial vs spatiotemporal.** H-T1-3 specifies a 3D-spatiotemporal FFT, but Yu et al. (Spectral Progressive Diffusion) note that the FFT exhibits oversmoothing artifacts compared to DCT or DWT for diffusion-spectrum analysis. Should we (a) use a 3D DFT (matches FADE CVPR 2025), (b) use a 3D DCT (matches VCA / video-coding precedent), or (c) compute both and store both bands? The latter at < 200 ms/video is essentially free.

3. **FLIPD timestep selection.** FLIPD's quality is sensitive to the noise level at which the score-and-trace is evaluated; the original paper uses a small t (so the noise-conditional score is close to the data score). For our 1000-step LongCat schedule, what is the equivalent "small t"? A pilot pass on 50 videos at t ∈ {20, 50, 100, 200} would settle this.

4. **Tier-3 mini-TTA sample-size.** H-T3-1 and H-T3-2 are both Tier-3, but H-T3-2's prediction (catastrophic-LoRA-tail screening) is a *conditional* prediction that targets the ~20 worst-performing videos under LoRA-r8 specifically. Should we run them on the *full* 999-video intersection, or a stratified subsample of 100 (50 random + 25 worst LoRA-r8 tail + 25 best LoRA-r8 tail)? The stratified version is 10× cheaper and arguably more diagnostic.

5. **VAE reconstruction error in pixel vs latent space.** H-T1-1 specifies pixel-space L1 + LPIPS for the round-trip error. Should we also store the latent-space L2 between the encoded clean latents and the encoded-then-noised-then-denoised latents at a small t? That second quantity is closer to the "model-internal" representation of the video and may correlate better with ΔPSNR. Cost is ~30% extra over pixel-space-only.
