## TL;DR

TTOM (ICLR 2026) reports a clear saturate-then-degrade curve when test-time optimization iterations push past a small soft optimum (peak at 8 iters/step in their setting; clear regression at 16) and attributes it to "excessive alignment optimization [that] may disrupt other essential signals". Their setting is qualitatively close to ours but mechanistically distinct on three axes — optimization variable, supervisory signal, and test-time loop scope — so TTOM does not foreclose our research question, but it does suggest one specific control (an explicit iteration sweep on a representative subsample) we should report rather than wave past.

## What TTOM actually claims

TTOM (Qu et al., ICLR 2026) is a training-free framework for *compositional* text-to-video generation that uses an LLM-derived spatiotemporal layout (per-object bounding-box sequence) as a controllable condition and aligns video-foundation-model cross-attention maps to that layout at inference time. The optimization variable is a rank-32 LoRA inserted into the Q/K/V/O projection layers of every cross-attention block in CogVideoX-5B and Wan2.1-14B; the loss is Jensen–Shannon divergence between extracted cross-attention maps and Gaussian-smoothed layout masks; the update is applied for only the first five denoising steps using AdamW at lr 1e-4 (TTOM, §3.2 and §4.1). Two saturation findings are reported in their §4.3:

- **Iterations per denoising step (their Table 8):** Motion score 0.3789 → 0.4321 (peak at 8 iters) → 0.4130 → 0.3547 (regression at 16 iters).
- **Number of guidance timesteps (their Table 7):** Motion score 0.2730 (1 step) → 0.3692 → 0.4321 (peak at 5 steps) → 0.4296 (mild regression at 7 steps).

Their attribution: "attention maps encode not only layout but also entangled information, and excessive alignment optimization may disrupt other essential signals" (TTOM, §4.3, last paragraph). A second contribution we note for completeness — a parametric memory that stores, retrieves, and updates layout-specific LoRA weights across a *stream* of user prompts (TTOM, §3.3) — is orthogonal to the saturation observation but does change the scope of the optimization loop relative to ours.

## Where our setting differs

Three mechanistically important axes; one axis where the comparison is genuinely close.

- **Optimization variable.** TTOM updates a rank-32 LoRA in cross-attention Q/K/V/O projections of a frozen ~5B / ~14B parameter cross-attention-bearing DiT. Our recipes span three different parameter families — AdaSteer (steering-style delta-tuning, ADA), LoRA r=8 (`LORA_R8_TTA`), and TinyLoRA r=2 (`TL_BARE_R2`, `TL_TIED_R2`) — applied to a substantially smaller LongCat-Video backbone (≈ 700 M params), and AdaSteer in particular is not a LoRA-class adapter at all (`REVIEW_per_video_tta_suitability_2026-06-09.md` §2). The "more iterations of the same LoRA" lever TTOM probes is not the same lever as "swap to a different adapter family"; TTOM's iteration-axis saturation does not directly imply saturation on our family-of-adapter axis.
- **Supervisory signal.** TTOM is *unsupervised* in the no-ground-truth-video sense: their loss is a JSD against an LLM-synthesized layout proxy. Our loss is a flow-matching MSE against *clean visible frames 0–47 of the held-out video itself* — a direct self-supervised signal from the data, not a generative-prior proxy (`REVIEW` §1; `PLAN_gating_experiment_2026-06-11.md` §1.4). This makes our optimization objective much more strongly informative per gradient step but also tightly bounded by what the visible-frames window contains.
- **Test-time loop scope.** TTOM accumulates adapted LoRA weights into a streaming parametric memory that is *reused across prompts* (TTOM, §3.3). Our adapter state resets between videos: every per-video TTA run starts from the same unadapted weights, isolating the per-video adaptation curve from cross-sample interference. TTOM's saturate-then-degrade observation therefore includes a memory-staleness component that ours, by construction, cannot.
- **Benchmark scale and evaluation metric.** TTOM evaluates on T2V-CompBench (~1400 compositional prompts, scored for motion / numeracy / spatial relation / consistent and dynamic attribute binding / action binding / object interactions) and the 16 semantic+quality dimensions of VBench. We evaluate on a 999-video Panda-70M intersection and a 932-video UCF-101 intersection at 480p, in both a 28-frame standard horizon and a 76-frame long-horizon regime, reporting PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS / FVD / FID alongside seven VBench dimensions (`REVIEW` §2.1; `INDEX.md` headline 1000v table). Aggregating across regimes the metric mix is reconstructive-fidelity-dominated, not compositional-alignment-dominated.
- **Where comparison is *close*, not different.** Both papers (i) use LoRA-style parametric adaptation injected into a frozen video DiT at test time; (ii) report saturation on at least one test-time-compute axis; and (iii) sit on essentially the same DiT-family architecture (cross-attention-bearing video DiTs). We should not claim our setting is "completely different" — it is close enough that a reviewer reading TTOM will reasonably expect us to engage with their saturation finding rather than ignore it.

## Draft positioning paragraph (paper-ready)

> A concurrent ICLR 2026 submission, TTOM (Qu et al., 2026), reports the closest analogue in recent literature to the saturation we observe: when test-time optimization iterations exceed a small soft optimum (eight per denoising step at five early timesteps in their setting), compositional video-generation quality first saturates and then *degrades*, with the authors attributing the regression to "excessive alignment optimization [that] may disrupt other essential signals" beyond the layout axis they target. We take this as the strongest prior evidence that test-time adaptation of large video DiTs can be self-limiting; three mechanistic differences nevertheless restrict the directness of any direct comparison. First, the *optimization variable*: TTOM trains a rank-32 LoRA inserted exclusively into cross-attention Q/K/V/O projections of CogVideoX-5B / Wan2.1-14B, updated for only the first five denoising steps via Jensen–Shannon divergence between cross-attention maps and Gaussian-smoothed LLM-generated layout masks (TTOM, §3.2); our recipes optimize three different adapter families (AdaSteer, LoRA r=8, TinyLoRA r=2) on a substantially smaller LongCat-Video backbone, against a flow-matching loss supervised by *clean visible frames of the held-out video itself* rather than by an LLM-synthesized layout proxy. Second, the *test-time loop scope*: TTOM accumulates adapted weights into a streaming parametric memory reused across prompts (TTOM, §3.3), so their saturate-then-degrade curve includes cross-sample interference; our adapter state resets between videos, isolating the per-video curve. Third, *what is aligned and how it is measured*: TTOM optimizes for compositional text–image alignment, scored on T2V-CompBench and the semantic dimensions of VBench, whereas we measure trajectory fidelity to the held-out continuation on PSNR / LPIPS / FVD plus seven VBench dimensions at 1000-video Panda-70M and 932-video UCF-101 scale (480p; 28- and 76-frame regimes). What TTOM does *not* foreclose, therefore, is whether per-video reconstructive TTA on long-horizon continuation saturates by over-optimization (their mechanism) or by failure to exceed a per-video noise floor (\|ΔPSNR\| ≤ 0.5 dB on 81–95 % of clips at standard horizon). What it *does* foreclose is the naïve "just run more iterations" defence: a small iteration sweep on a representative subsample is therefore a control we report rather than wave past.

## What an AdaSteer reader should take away

- **TTOM does NOT foreclose our research question.** The supervisory signal (clean visible frames vs. LLM-layout JSD), the optimization variable (three adapter families incl. non-LoRA AdaSteer vs. one fixed cross-attention LoRA), and the test-time loop scope (per-video reset vs. cross-prompt memory) are all mechanistically distinct enough that their iteration-axis degradation does not transfer term-for-term to ours.
- **TTOM does inform our analysis.** Saturate-then-degrade is a documented real phenomenon in the closest neighbouring setting, so our paper must show it has been tested for — not assert by silence that it could not occur.
- **What we should NOT claim.** Both methods are LoRA-style adapters at test time on cross-attention-bearing video DiTs; both report saturation on a test-time-compute axis. We should not write that the settings are "completely different" — they are close enough that a reviewer will reasonably expect engagement, and overclaiming difference is a tactically weak defence.

## Specific control TTOM suggests we add

**Vary TTA iterations on a representative ≈ 100-video subsample and check explicitly for a degradation crossover.** Concretely: for each of {ADA, LORA_R8_TTA, TL_BARE_R2}, sweep `--tta-steps ∈ {10, 20, 40, 80, 160}` on a stratified 100-video Panda-1000v subset (mixed across the `chunk_idx` partition used by `PLAN_gating_experiment_2026-06-11.md` §3.3 leave-one-chunk-out CV) and plot mean ΔPSNR / ΔLPIPS / ΔFVD vs. iteration count, including the 16-iter regression point as a deliberate over-shoot. **Cost:** at ~5 min/video per TTA chunk on H200 and ~5 iter-points × 3 methods × 100 videos = 1500 TTA runs ≈ 125 GPU-h serial; well below the cost of a single 999-video headline sweep and below the ~220 GPU-h budgeted for the priority-1–4 recipe modifications in `LITERATURE_tta_recipe_modifications_2026-06-12.md` §1. **Outcome interpretation:** if we *see* the TTOM-style crossover (peak then regression at higher iters), the paper claim becomes "our saturation has the same mechanism as TTOM's"; if we *do not* see it (monotonic flat curve), the paper claim becomes "our saturation is at the per-video noise floor, mechanistically distinct from TTOM's over-optimization saturation" — both outcomes are paper-defensible.

## What we cannot confidently distinguish

These are the close-comparison axes; do **not** claim difference here unless the user explicitly wants the rhetorical push:

- **Adapter architecture.** Both papers inject LoRA (or LoRA-like) parameters into a frozen video DiT. Our LORA_R8_TTA family is directly analogous to their rank-32 LoRA, only at lower rank and on a smaller backbone. (AdaSteer and TinyLoRA are honestly different; LORA_R8_TTA is not.)
- **Saturation existence.** Both papers report saturation on a test-time-compute axis. The *mechanism* may differ (theirs: over-alignment vs. ours: noise floor) but the *phenomenon* is shared. Claim differing mechanism, not differing existence.
- **Test-time DiT optimization in general.** Both papers are in the small recent literature that updates DiT-attached parameters at inference time (cf. CustomTTT, Bi et al., AAAI 2025, also LoRA-on-DiT). The category overlap is real.
