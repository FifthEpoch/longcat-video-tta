# Literature pass: TTA recipe modifications worth trying

**Date:** 2026-06-12
**Context:** The current AdaSteer/LoRA/TinyLoRA recipes saturate on Panda 1000v / 480p. Population gain ≈ 0; per-video gain prediction shows |Spearman ρ| ≤ 0.09 on data-intrinsic features. Before concluding the saturation is fundamental, we should test whether RECIPE modifications (not just gating) can break it.

## TL;DR

Five recipe modifications are worth queuing behind gating Phase 0–3, drawn from five distinct themes of the recent diffusion-TTA literature. **Priority 1 is the anchor-frame consistency loss** — frames 0–47 are clean ground truth that the current TTA loss ignores, and a `pred_x0 ↔ visible_frames` MSE/LPIPS auxiliary term is a ≤80-LOC bolt-on to `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` with broad literature support (CustomTTT AAAI 2025, x0-supervision CVPR 2026, MiVID 2025, Video Diffusion Models reconstruction guidance NeurIPS 2022). Priority 2 is **VAE-decoder-only TTA**, a *recipe pivot* that adapts the bottleneck the user already suspects is the binding constraint (REPA-E ICCV 2025, LeanVAE ICCV 2025). **No published work shows clean per-video TTA gains on a 1000-video, 480p video-DiT benchmark using only the conditioning frames as supervision** — the closest comparable result (CustomTTT) needs a separately-trained reference LoRA, not a single test video.

## 1. Why we're looking

REVIEW §2.1 establishes population-level saturation at Panda 1000v / 480p / 17-frame standard horizon: AdaSteer / LoRA-r8 / TinyLoRA-r2 all land within 0.1 PSNR / 8 FVD of NOTTA. Per-video ΔPSNR is dominated by sampling noise (~95 % of clips within ±0.5 dB for TinyLoRA), and three data-intrinsic features (RAFT mean-flow, baseline PSNR, caption length) show |Spearman ρ| ≤ 0.09 — they predict nothing. The gating experiment (PLAN_gating_experiment_2026-06-11.md, AUTHORISED) tests whether *model-aware* features (Tier-2 diffusion-OOD, Tier-3 mini-TTA probes) gate the existing recipes better. That experiment leaves a parallel question untested: *can a different TTA recipe break the saturation in the first place*, independent of any gate? The three recipes we have tested are all variants of **flow-matching MSE on noised conditioning latents** (`compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` in `delta_experiment/scripts/common.py`); the literature offers ten distinct recipe families we have not tried.

Themes searched: (1) TTA-for-diffusion-models specifically (2024–2026), (2) latent-space-only TTA (VAE-encoder / decoder-only adaptation), (3) anchor-frame consistency / hybrid losses, (4) augmentation-consistency TTA (MEMO / TTT-MAE style), (5) CFG-aware TTA / per-sample CFG scheduling, (6) test-time prompt engineering and ensembling, (7) iterative / curriculum / annealed-timestep TTA, (8) meta-learning / amortized TTA (HyperDreamBooth-style), (9) continual streaming TTA across the test stream, (10) recent video-diffusion-specific TTA (CVPR/ICCV/NeurIPS 2024–2026). Each theme was probed with 3–5 keyword combinations and ≥1 paper read in full.

## 2. What the recent literature shows

### Theme 1 — TTA-for-diffusion-models specifically (2024–2026)

The recent TTA-on-diffusion literature splits cleanly into two camps. **Camp A** (Diffusion-TTA, Prabhudesai et al. NeurIPS 2023; DUSA, Han et al. 2024) uses a *frozen* diffusion model as a generative prior to adapt a *discriminative* model at test time — the opposite of our setting, but operationally interesting because it confirms that diffusion likelihood gradients are a viable training signal. **Camp B** (Null-TTA, Kim et al. 2025; SteeringTTA, 2025; SDA, CVPR 2025) does *test-time alignment* of the diffusion model itself, but optimises only the null-text embedding (CFG anchor), the noise trajectory, or input pixels — never the model weights. The single recent paper that *fine-tunes* video-diffusion weights at test time on per-video data is **CustomTTT** (Bi et al., AAAI 2025; [project](https://customttt.github.io/), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15646)), and it does so to *combine* two pre-trained LoRA adapters (motion + appearance), not to adapt to a single fresh video — the TTA losses are distillation-from-teacher (Lap, Ltp), not self-supervised reconstruction. The other 2024–2026 result is **TTT layers for one-minute video generation** (Dalal et al., CVPR 2025; [PDF](https://test-time-training.github.io/video-dit/assets/ttt_cvpr_2025.pdf)) — but TTT layers are *trained into the model* at pre-training and live inside each attention block, they are not a test-time-only adaptation. **Bottom line for Theme 1:** there is no published work showing that adapting the DiT weights with the conditioning frames as the only supervisory signal produces per-video gain on a 1000-video benchmark. CustomTTT is the closest comparable, and the supervisory signal it actually uses (teacher distillation) is fundamentally different from ours.

A second 2026 result, **TTOM** ("Test-Time Optimization with Memory", [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=wqCwcTZsrv)), is relevant context: when they push test-time optimization iterations beyond a saturation point, performance *degrades* because over-optimization disrupts "essential signals". Their setting (attention-map alignment for layout control) is not our setting, but the saturation-then-degradation pattern echoes our finding and confirms that "more TTA iterations" is not by itself a recipe modification worth testing — the iteration count is already at the soft optimum across our three recipes.

### Theme 2 — Latent-space-only TTA (VAE encoder / decoder, not the DiT)

Two ICCV 2025 papers reframe the latent-space adaptation question: **REPA-E** (Leng et al., [ICCV 2025 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Leng_REPA-E_Unlocking_VAE_for_End-to-End_Tuning_of_Latent_Diffusion_Transformers_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), [code](https://github.com/End2End-Diffusion/REPA-E)) demonstrates that VAE + LDM can be co-tuned end-to-end, but only if the diffusion-loss gradient through the VAE is replaced by a representation-alignment loss; vanilla diffusion-loss-into-VAE degrades performance. This is a strong negative result for the naive recipe "just unfreeze the VAE and add it to the trainable parameter list under the existing TTA loss" — it is unlikely to help, and may hurt. **LeanVAE** (Cheng et al., ICCV 2025; [code](https://github.com/westlake-repl/leanvae)) is a small (40 M-parameter) video VAE that decodes a 17-frame 1080p clip in 0.9 s and is light enough that *full* VAE fine-tuning at test time is GPU-cheap. Neither paper addresses test-time adaptation specifically, but together they license a focused recipe pivot: **adapt only the VAE decoder, on the VAE round-trip reconstruction objective, with the DiT frozen**. The user's hypothesis from REVIEW §4.1 ("the transformer is too capable to need adaptation; the bottleneck is the VAE round-trip") is exactly the question this experiment tests, and the per-video adaptation budget shifts from 700 M (LongCat DiT, even with rank-8 LoRA) to ≤ 50 M (the VAE decoder), making convergence in the same 10 TTA steps far more plausible.

### Theme 3 — Anchor-frame consistency / hybrid losses (the priority-1 modification)

This is the modification the user flagged as the lowest-hanging fruit, and the literature broadly confirms it. **Video Diffusion Models** (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2022; [PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/39235c56aef13fb05a6adc95eb9d8d66-Paper-Conference.pdf)) introduces "reconstruction guidance" for video continuation: at each sampling step, add a gradient term that pulls the prediction toward agreement with the conditioning frames in pixel space. **Diffusion Models for Video Prediction and Infilling / RaMViD** (Höppe et al., 2022; [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.07696)) formalises the dual-region loss `J_t(θ) = ‖s_θ(x_t^U ⊕ x_0^C) − ∇log p(x_t^U | x_0)‖²` where the supervisory signal is computed *only* on the unknown frames but the known frames provide clean conditioning — this is structurally what we do, with the loss restricted to the noised half. **MiVID** (Mishra et al., 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06019v1)) extends this with a *pixel-level* reconstruction MSE *on top of* the diffusion loss for self-supervised frame interpolation: `L_total = L_diffusion + λ_recon · L_pixel + λ_perceptual · L_LPIPS`, with progressive mask injection. **x0-supervision** (Sangare et al., CVPR 2026; [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Sangare_Improving_Controllable_Generation_Faster_Training_and_Better_Performance_via_x0-Supervision_CVPR_2026_paper.html)) shows that for controllable generation (which is what conditioning-frame generation is), training with `‖x0_pred − x0_GT‖²` instead of `‖ε_pred − ε‖²` accelerates convergence by up to 2× — the noise-prediction objective implicitly down-weights global-structure gradients that x0-prediction does not. **FlowLoss** (Xia et al., 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14535)) shows that adding an optical-flow consistency auxiliary loss between the denoised prediction and ground truth on robotic video data accelerates convergence and improves motion coherence. Across this literature, the auxiliary-loss weight λ varies wildly (MiVID does not report a clean number; FlowLoss uses `s = 10⁻⁶` for a flow term scaled by the EDM weighting `σ²+1/σ²`; controllable-generation x0-supervision recommends weighting `1/SNR(t)` to match the implicit ε-loss weighting at uniform-timestep training), so a sweep is required.

The recipe modification this implies is concrete and small: at every TTA step, in addition to the current `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` MSE on noised `train_latents`, also compute `pred_x0_train = (z_t − σ · v_pred) / α` from the velocity prediction and add an auxiliary `‖pred_x0_train − train_latents‖²` *or* a perceptual-decoded variant `‖VAE.decode(pred_x0_train) − pixel_frames_train‖²` (an LPIPS or pixel MSE). The visible frames are clean ground truth already encoded by the VAE earlier in the runner, so the marginal cost is one VAE decode per TTA step (≈ 50–150 ms on H200 at 480p × 12 latent frames) for the perceptual variant, or zero extra forward passes for the pure-latent variant.

### Theme 4 — Augmentation-consistency TTA (MEMO / TTT-MAE style)

**MEMO** (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09506), [code](https://github.com/zhangmarvin/memo)) — the canonical augmentation-consistency TTA — generates K random augmentations of the single test input, computes the model's average (marginal) output, and adapts the model parameters to minimise the entropy of that marginal. The translation to generative diffusion is non-trivial because there is no discrete label to compute entropy over, but the *consistency* form survives cleanly: the model's denoised x0 prediction on augmented inputs should agree (after un-augmenting). **TTT-MAE** (Gandelsman et al., NeurIPS 2022; [project](https://yossigandelsman.github.io/ttt_mae/index.html), [code](https://github.com/yossigandelsman/test_time_training_mae)) makes this even cleaner for our setting: at test time, mask 75 % of patches and adapt the model to reconstruct them — the bias-variance theorem says the adapted model has higher variance but lower bias, and the trade-off is favourable. **VideoMAE** (Tong et al., NeurIPS 2022; [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=AhccnBXSne)) extends to video with 90–95 % tube-mask ratios, which is the natural way to apply TTT-MAE-style adaptation to a 28-frame clip. The augmentation-consistency line is well-supported in classification (1–8 % gains on ImageNet-C / R / A) but has not been tested on video-diffusion latents.

What is *already in* the codebase but not used as a consistency signal: `build_augmented_pixel_variants` (`common.py:1332`) already builds horizontal-flip / rotation / speed-factor variants and encodes each into a separate latent. The runners pass these variants as *independent training samples* (`tinylora_run.py:584` — round-robin over variants per step), not as inputs to a consistency loss. A MEMO-style modification swaps the loss: at each TTA step, compute the velocity prediction on each augmented variant, *un-augment* the predictions to a common reference frame, and minimise the cross-augmentation variance of the resulting predicted-x0 latents. This is small in implementation and exploits infrastructure that already exists.

### Theme 5 — CFG-aware TTA / classifier-free guidance manipulation

Three recent papers establish that CFG is *strongly per-sample*: **Limited Interval Guidance** (Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2024; [PDF](https://users.aalto.fi/~laines9/publications/kynkaanniemi2024guidance_paper.pdf), [poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93711)) shows guidance is *harmful* at high σ and *unnecessary* at low σ — restricting it to a middle interval `[σ_lo, σ_hi]` improves ImageNet-512 FID from 1.81 to 1.40. **β-CFG** (Polewski et al., 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10574), [code](https://github.com/gmum/beta-CFG)) replaces the constant guidance weight with a `β(t)` distribution-modulated curve and reports consistently better FID. **Dynamic CFG via Online Feedback** (Imagen team, ICLR 2026 submission; [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z9YC9bvfUL); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16131)) shows that per-prompt, per-timestep CFG selection via greedy search against latent-space evaluators wins 53.8 % human-preference vs Imagen 3 default. None of these is test-time *adaptation* — they all sweep CFG at inference for a *frozen* model — but they license the recipe pivot: replace our constant `--guidance-scale 4.0` with a *per-video* learned CFG schedule, fit on the visible frames (e.g., during TTA, also optimise a small `w(σ)` MLP head against the same anchor-frame reconstruction signal from Theme 3). This is a Theme-3 + Theme-5 hybrid and would be a natural follow-up if Theme 3 alone moves the needle.

The simpler version — without learning a per-video CFG — is "set CFG=1 outside `[σ_lo, σ_hi]` and CFG=4 inside". For Panda 1000v this is a single sbatch flag plus a 4-line patch to the `generate_video_continuation` call. Worth a Phase-1-style sweep alongside (not instead of) the recipe pivots.

### Theme 6 — Test-time prompt engineering / ensembling

This theme is broadly *negative* for our setting. The literature is dominated by **DiffTPT** (Feng et al., ICCV 2023; [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Feng_Diverse_Data_Augmentation_with_Diffusions_for_Effective_Test-time_Prompt_Tuning_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)), **IT³A** (Liang et al., 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09706)), and **Cap2Aug** (Roy et al., 2022; [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05404)) — all generate BLIP / Stable Diffusion augmentations for *classification* test-time prompt tuning. Their gains (~5 % on zero-shot accuracy) don't translate to our setting because (a) our backbone is a generator not a classifier, (b) the NOPROMPT ablation (REVIEW §2.2) already proved the TTA-time caption is a noise channel for Panda standard horizon (|ρ(Δ, caption_words)| ≈ −0.02), so adding *more* captions via BLIP would compound that noise. **Decision: skip this theme.** The infrastructure-only version (run BLIP-2 to generate alternative captions, ensemble at inference time) is a viable add at zero TTA-recipe cost but it doesn't change the saturation question.

### Theme 7 — Iterative / curriculum / annealed-timestep TTA

**Diffusion Time-step Curriculum** (Yi et al., CVPR 2024; [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Yi_Diffusion_Time-step_Curriculum_for_One_Image_to_3D_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04562v3)) is the cleanest application. Their **annealed time-step schedule** samples `t ∈ [t_mid − Δ, t_mid + Δ]` per training step, with `t_mid` monotonically decreasing from `t_max` to `t_min` and `Δ` narrowing. The mechanism: diffusion is coarse-to-fine, so early TTA iterations should optimise large-timestep (global-structure) loss and later iterations should refine small-timestep (texture-detail) loss. They report substantial improvement on image-to-3D over uniform-timestep sampling. Our current TTA loss uses uniform `σ ~ U(σ_min, σ_max)` per step (`compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned`, `common.py:439`); a one-line modification to bias the σ-sampling toward `t_mid(k)` (where `k` is the TTA-step index) is a small-cost easy add. **Restart sampling** (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2023; [PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/f2543511e5f4d4764857f9ad833a977d-Paper-Conference.pdf)) is a related-but-orthogonal recipe for *sampling*, not training.

A second iterative angle: **SGD-ER** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04117)) and warm-restart cosine schedules. We currently use a flat AdamW LR for the 10–20 TTA steps. The literature does not strongly support a particular schedule for TTA-on-diffusion specifically, but the cost of testing two or three LR schedules is one extra sbatch wrapper. Lower-priority than the timestep curriculum.

### Theme 8 — Meta-learning / amortized TTA

**MetaDiff** (Zhang et al., AAAI 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16424v2)) and **HyperDreamBooth** (Ruiz et al., 2023; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.06949v2)) train a hypernetwork that predicts per-subject LoRA weights in a single forward pass, eliminating the per-subject fine-tuning loop. **Finetuning-Free Hypernetwork Personalization** (Yang et al., 2025; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03156v1)) and **HyperNet Fields** (Hedlin et al., 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17040)) are 2024–2025 extensions that train without needing precomputed per-subject ground-truth LoRA weights. The case for trying this in our setting: a hypernetwork trained on the 999-video Panda set (with held-out 5-fold CV) to predict the LoRA-r8 weights from the visible-frame CLIP/DINO features could (a) converge to a *better* per-video LoRA than the current 10-step Adam, (b) confer a 100×+ wallclock saving, (c) provide a learned per-video "expected gain" signal that *is* the gate (no separate feature-extraction pass needed).

The case against: this is the most expensive modification by far. Training the hypernetwork is a new pre-training pass requiring ground-truth LoRAs for ~800 videos (the 5-fold training partition), each of which is itself a ~10 GPU-min run — so ~130 GPU-h of generated training data plus the hypernetwork training itself. Cost classification: large. Not on the immediate post-gating list, but the natural "if Theme 3 + Theme 4 both fail to break saturation, the diagnosis is that the supervisory signal is fundamentally too weak — and amortising over the whole dataset is the way to inject more signal" follow-up.

### Theme 9 — Continual / streaming TTA

**Online TTT for Video Streams** (Wang, Sun, Gandelsman et al., JMLR 2025; [PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume26/24-0439/24-0439.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05014v1), [project](https://video-ttt.github.io/)) is the most relevant. Their result: not resetting the model parameters between video frames — **implicit memory** — gives >2× and 1.5× improvements over the fixed-model baseline for instance and panoptic segmentation. Online TTT *also outperforms* offline TTT (which trains a single model per *full* test video) — a counter-intuitive locality finding. **CoTTA** (Wang et al., CVPR 2022; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13591)) handles the obvious risk (catastrophic forgetting on a non-stationary stream) by *stochastic weight restoration*: at each step, with probability `p`, restore each parameter to its source-pretrained value. **EATA** (Niu et al., ICML 2022) handles it by filtering out unreliable high-entropy samples and adding a weight-importance regulariser.

Translation to our setting: currently `tinylora_run.py:469` resets the v-vectors to zero between videos (`wrapper.reset_v()`); the LoRA runner similarly reinitialises adapters per video. The Online-TTT modification is: **don't reset**; initialise from the previous video's adapted state and adapt on the current video's visible frames. The risk is high (Panda 1000v is hash-ordered random sampling, so adjacent videos are not topically correlated — the wrong setting for vanilla online TTT). The CoTTA mitigation makes it workable, but the tuning of `p` is non-trivial. Medium-cost recipe pivot worth one budgeted slot.

### Theme 10 — Recent CVPR/ICCV/NeurIPS 2024–2026 video-diffusion-specific

Reviewed above as cross-cutting context. The key 2025–2026 video-diffusion TTA work is **CustomTTT** (Theme 1 + Theme 3, AAAI 2025), **One-Minute Video Generation with TTT** (Dalal et al., CVPR 2025 — TTT layers in pre-training, not in-scope), **Video-T1** (Liu et al., ICCV 2025; [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Liu_Video-T1_Test-time_Scaling_for_Video_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), [project](https://liuff19.github.io/Video-T1/)) and its streaming variant **Stream-T1** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04461)) which are about test-time *scaling* (more sampling compute, tree-of-frames search), not test-time *adaptation*, and **TTOM** (saturation observation noted above). **CVPR 2026 Workshop VGBE: Test-Time Domain Adaptation for Interactive Video Generation** (Rawal & Kumar, [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=FRcX8bJwVQ)) addresses internal-covariate-shift from mask-conditioning at test time and is the closest 2026 paper to our setting in spirit, but its mechanism (Mask Normalization + Temporal Intrinsic Denoising) targets the masking-conditioning gap in interactive generation rather than per-video weight adaptation. No 2026 paper directly forecloses our research question.

## 3. Selected modifications

Five modifications, spanning five themes, with one MANDATORY priority-1 modification (anchor-frame loss, Theme 3) and a healthy easy-add / recipe-pivot mix.

### Modification 1: Anchor-Frame x0 Consistency Loss

- **Statement:** Add an auxiliary `‖pred_x0_train − train_latents‖²` (or a VAE-decoded perceptual `‖VAE.decode(pred_x0_train) − pixel_frames_train‖²`) term to the TTA loss, weighted by `λ_anchor`. The visible frames are clean ground truth already encoded by the VAE earlier in the runner; we currently compute the flow-matching MSE on noised latents only and discard the free supervisory signal that the predicted x0 must equal the known clean latent.
- **Mechanism:** The current epsilon/velocity loss `‖v_pred − v_true‖²` is asymptotically equivalent to the x0 loss `‖x0_pred − x0_true‖²` up to an `(α/σ)²` per-σ weighting that down-weights global-structure gradients at low SNR (Sangare et al. CVPR 2026 demonstrate this explicitly). For per-video adaptation, where global structure (the specific subject identity, scene layout, dominant motion of *this video*) is exactly what we want the adapter to capture, the x0 weighting is the right one. Adding the x0 term restores the global-structure signal. The VAE-decoded variant additionally provides a *perceptual* gradient that the latent-only loss does not (MiVID Mishra et al. 2025; FlowLoss Xia et al. 2025). This breaks the saturation if the saturation is caused by the TTA loss converging on a noised-latent objective that is roughly invariant to the actual visible-frame content.
- **Cost:** small. Pure-latent variant: ≤ 30-line patch to `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` (`common.py:439`) — compute `pred_x0 = (z_t − σ · v_pred) / (1 − σ)` (LongCat flow-matching parameterisation) and add MSE against `target_latents`. VAE-decoded variant: ≤ 80 LOC adding one decode call per TTA step (≈ 50–150 ms / step on H200 for 12 latent frames at 480p), and an LPIPS module (already a dependency). One new sbatch wrapper or a `--anchor-loss-weight` flag in existing runners.
- **Expected effect:** Most likely outcome: unlocks a small but real per-video gain on the **AdaSteer family** (the loosest tail, ~81 % within ±0.5 dB — REVIEW §2.1) where the current loss is least confident; smaller gain on LoRA-r8 (already over-confident, demonstrated by `panda_0098` 22 dB regression — REVIEW §2.4); and possibly *no* gain on TinyLoRA-r2 (only 2 trainable parameters per layer — the recipe is parameter-bound, not signal-bound). The hypothesis it would falsify: that adapting the DiT with conditioning frames is the binding constraint. If anchor-loss buys nothing, the constraint is elsewhere (most likely the VAE — see Modification 2).
- **Citation (primary):** Sangare et al., "Improving Controllable Generation: Faster Training and Better Performance via x0-Supervision", CVPR 2026, [openaccess](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Sangare_Improving_Controllable_Generation_Faster_Training_and_Better_Performance_via_x0-Supervision_CVPR_2026_paper.html); secondary: Höppe et al., "Diffusion Models for Video Prediction and Infilling (RaMViD)", 2022, [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.07696); Mishra et al., "MiVID: Multi-Strategic Self-Supervision for Video Frame Interpolation using Diffusion Model", 2025, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06019v1); Bi et al., "CustomTTT", AAAI 2025, [project](https://customttt.github.io/).
- **Falsification:** held-out per-video ΔPSNR distribution under `ADA + anchor-loss` is statistically indistinguishable from `ADA` baseline (Wilcoxon signed-rank `p > 0.10`, |median Δ shift| < 0.05 dB) across the full Panda 1000v at any `λ_anchor ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0}` *and* the same pattern holds for the perceptual-decoded variant. This would specifically rule out the "free supervisory signal" hypothesis; if it falsifies, the bottleneck is not the TTA loss formulation.
- **Priority: 1.** This is the first modification to implement after gating Phase 0–3 RECOMMENDATION.md lands.
- **Implementation status:** **implemented** in the anchor-frame x0 loss landing commit (2026-06-12; pure-latent variant; commit hash visible via `git log --grep="anchor-frame x0 consistency loss" -1 --pretty=format:%H` on the cluster checkout); awaiting smoke-test on cluster restart. The patch added `anchor_x0_weight` (default 0.0 = byte-identical to pre-patch, verified by a regression test on `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned`) to all three TTA runners (`run_tinylora.py`, `run_delta_a.py`, `run_lora_tta.py`) and to all three sbatch wrappers (`run_sweep.sbatch`, `run_tinylora.sbatch`, `run_lora_tta.sbatch`) via the `ANCHOR_X0_WEIGHT` env-var. Smoke-test wrapper `sweep_experiment/sbatch/submit_smoke_x0_loss.sh` fires a single chunk of `LORA_R8_TTA` at λ=1.0 (~2 GPU h on H200); cluster-restart procedure per `INDEX.md` "Implemented but not yet run (recipe modifications)" section. The VAE-decoded perceptual variant is deferred — pure-latent first; the perceptual term can be a follow-up if the latent variant clears the noise-floor bar.

### Modification 2: VAE-Decoder-Only TTA

- **Statement:** Freeze the DiT entirely. Adapt only the LongCat-Video VAE *decoder* weights, with the TTA loss `‖VAE.decode(VAE.encode(pixel_frames_train)) − pixel_frames_train‖²_pix + λ_lpips · LPIPS(.)`. Generation proceeds normally afterward with the per-video-tuned decoder.
- **Mechanism:** The user's prior (REVIEW §4.1) is that the DiT is "too capable to need adaptation" and the round-trip through the VAE is what costs the per-video fidelity. If the prior is correct, the binding constraint is the VAE's per-video reconstruction fidelity, not the DiT's prediction. The recipe pivot makes the VAE decoder per-video — for video content with unusual statistics (cartoons, text-on-screen, ultra-high-frequency texture: exactly the `panda_0461 / 0555 / 0862` cohort that all methods recover under REVIEW §2.3), the per-video VAE decoder will have substantially better reconstruction. REPA-E (Leng et al. ICCV 2025) demonstrates that VAE adaptation has measurable downstream generation impact when paired with the right regulariser; LeanVAE (Cheng et al. ICCV 2025) demonstrates that VAE-only fine-tuning is computationally cheap (a 40 M-param video VAE decodes 17 frames 1080p in 0.9 s).
- **Cost:** small / medium. Needs a new runner (`scripts/run_vae_decoder_tta.py`) but reuses `load_longcat_components` and `encode_video` from `common.py`. No DiT forward pass during TTA. Memory savings should be large (LongCat DiT is ~30 GB activations at 28 frames; VAE decoder is ~3 GB), allowing larger TTA-step counts (50–100 steps in the same wallclock as 10 DiT steps). Two sbatch wrappers (`run_vae_only_tta.sbatch` for Panda standard horizon and one for long horizon).
- **Expected effect:** Predicted to deliver the largest per-video PSNR uplift of any modification on **video-types where the VAE round-trip is the bottleneck**: text-on-white-background, cartoon, dragon-ball-Z-style (the §2.3 cohort). Predicted *no* gain on visually generic content where the VAE already round-trips at low error. This is the modification that would best support a paper subsection titled "the bottleneck is the VAE, not the DiT" with hard empirical evidence; conversely, if it fails, the bottleneck is somewhere else (DiT capacity, prompt conditioning, or fundamentally the test-time supervisory signal). **Important caveat:** the VAE-decoded `pred_x0` from Modification 1 produces the same effect partially; if Modification 1 already delivers, Modification 2's marginal benefit shrinks. Worth running Modifications 1 and 2 independently then as an additive combination.
- **Citation (primary):** Leng et al., "REPA-E: Unlocking VAE for End-to-End Tuning with Latent Diffusion Transformers", ICCV 2025, [paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Leng_REPA-E_Unlocking_VAE_for_End-to-End_Tuning_of_Latent_Diffusion_Transformers_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf), [code](https://github.com/End2End-Diffusion/REPA-E); secondary: Cheng et al., "LeanVAE: An Ultra-Efficient Reconstruction VAE for Video Diffusion Models", ICCV 2025, [code](https://github.com/westlake-repl/leanvae).
- **Falsification:** held-out ΔPSNR on the four §2.3 beneficiary videos (`panda_0461`, `panda_0555`, `panda_0862`, `panda_0431`) under VAE-decoder TTA does *not* exceed +1.0 dB on at least 3 of 4. These are the cleanest test cases — if the recipe doesn't help here, it won't help on the saturated middle of the distribution.
- **Priority: 2.** The most aggressive recipe pivot — and the one whose result most directly tests the user's pre-existing structural hypothesis about LongCat-Video's bottleneck.
- **Implementation status:** **implemented** in the VAE-decoder-only TTA landing commit (2026-06-13; pure pixel-MSE round-trip variant; commit hash visible via `git log --grep="VAE-decoder-only TTA" -1 --pretty=format:%H` on the cluster checkout). The new runner [`delta_experiment/scripts/run_vae_decoder_tta.py`](../../delta_experiment/scripts/run_vae_decoder_tta.py) freezes the DiT entirely, snapshots the pristine VAE decoder once at load time, optimises `vae.decoder.parameters()` with AdamW (default lr=1e-5, 10 steps, grad-clip=1.0) on a per-video pixel-MSE round-trip loss, then restores the decoder from snapshot at the end of every video so adapter state never carries across the stream. Decoder drift `||Δw||_2` is logged per-video for diagnostics. Dispatch wired via new `METHOD=vae_decoder` case in [`sweep_experiment/sbatch/run_sweep.sbatch`](../sbatch/run_sweep.sbatch), gated by env vars `VAE_TTA_STEPS / VAE_TTA_LR / VAE_TTA_LPIPS_WEIGHT / VAE_TTA_GRAD_CLIP / VAE_TTA_WEIGHT_DECAY`. Smoke-test wrapper [`sweep_experiment/sbatch/submit_smoke_vae_decoder_tta.sh`](../sbatch/submit_smoke_vae_decoder_tta.sh) fires a single chunk of `VAE_DEC_TTA_LR1e-5` on Panda 1000v `chunk_0` matching the headline 28-frame standard horizon geometry (~3–5 GPU h on H200, sbatch cap 12 h). The optional LPIPS-decoded variant is wired but defaults off (`VAE_TTA_LPIPS_WEIGHT=0.0`); enabling it requires the `lpips` package and adds ~50–150 ms / TTA step on H200. Mod 2 was selected as the post-D1 PIVOT after the Modification 1 smoke-test returned median ΔPSNR = +0.0093 dB (below the 0.05 dB threshold; ANALYSIS_LOG D1 verdict 2026-06-13 fires) — the loss formulation was not the binding constraint, leaving "the VAE round-trip is" as the next most-likely structural hypothesis to test.

### Modification 3: Augmentation-Consistency TTA (MEMO / TTT-MAE style)

- **Statement:** Replace the current per-step round-robin over augmented variants (where each variant is used as an independent training sample) with a *consistency loss*: at each TTA step, compute the model's velocity prediction on K augmented variants of the same visible-frame clip, un-augment each prediction to the canonical frame, and minimise the cross-augmentation L2 distance of the resulting `pred_x0` latents. This is MEMO's marginal-entropy-minimisation idea adapted to a continuous-output regressor: the model should make the same prediction across augmentations.
- **Mechanism:** MEMO and TTT-MAE consistently deliver 1–8 % gains in classification under distribution shift by enforcing augmentation invariance. The corresponding hypothesis here: the per-video TTA loss currently overfits to *one* observation of the visible frames; cross-augmentation consistency provides a free regulariser that prevents overfitting to the specific noise / sampling realisation of those frames and forces the adapter to capture invariants. For videos with strong temporal structure (high RAFT mean-flow, which our null-result H1 in REVIEW §3 says is uncorrelated with current TTA gain — but that null is *under the current recipe*), the speed-factor augmentations in particular should provide useful gradient diversity. Augmentation infrastructure already exists (`build_augmented_pixel_variants`, `common.py:1332`).
- **Cost:** small. Modify `optimize_tinylora` / equivalent in the LoRA runner to compute K velocity predictions per step (with `K ≤ 4`) instead of one, and add a `1/K(K-1) · Σ_{i<j} ‖pred_x0_i − pred_x0_j‖²` consistency loss term to the existing flow-matching loss. Per-step cost goes from 1 forward + 1 backward to K forward + 1 backward (since the consistency loss requires keeping all K forward predictions in the graph). With `K=2`, the wallclock cost is roughly 2× current — manageable inside the 10-step TTA budget.
- **Expected effect:** Modest. The augmentation-consistency loss adds a regulariser that may stabilise the AdaSteer tails (the loosest family) and slightly improve TinyLoRA bias, but is unlikely to *break* the saturation on its own. Its value is as a multiplier on top of Modification 1: the anchor-frame loss provides the absolute signal, the consistency loss prevents the adapter from overfitting to a specific augmentation realisation of that signal. Combine with Modification 1 for the strongest test.
- **Citation (primary):** Zhang et al., "MEMO: Test Time Robustness via Adaptation and Augmentation", NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09506), [code](https://github.com/zhangmarvin/memo); secondary: Gandelsman et al., "Test-Time Training with Masked Autoencoders (TTT-MAE)", NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07522), [project](https://yossigandelsman.github.io/ttt_mae/index.html).
- **Falsification:** held-out per-video ΔPSNR distribution under `ADA + consistency-loss (K=2)` is *not* tighter (lower σ) than `ADA` baseline at population level by at least 0.1 dB, *and* the |Δ| ≤ 0.5 dB clip fraction does not improve by ≥ 2 percentage points. If the consistency loss isn't even tightening the tails, the augmentations are not providing meaningful gradient diversity.
- **Priority: 3.** Implement after Modifications 1 and 2 produce baseline signal; layer on top.

### Modification 4: Annealed-Timestep Curriculum TTA

- **Statement:** Replace uniform-σ sampling in `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` with a per-TTA-step annealed schedule `σ ~ U(σ_mid(k) − Δ(k), σ_mid(k) + Δ(k))`, where `σ_mid(k)` monotonically decreases from `σ_max` to `σ_min` across the TTA steps and `Δ(k)` narrows. Optionally couple with limited-interval guidance at inference time (CFG=4 inside `[σ_lo, σ_hi]`, CFG=1 elsewhere).
- **Mechanism:** Diffusion is coarse-to-fine. Per the Yi et al. CVPR 2024 diffusion time-step curriculum: large σ governs global structure, small σ governs fine detail. The current uniform-σ TTA loss averages gradients across timesteps that are doing *different* things; the adapter is asked to simultaneously be a better global-structure model and a better detail model, with no curriculum. For per-video adaptation specifically, the first few TTA steps should bias toward large-σ (learning *this video's* global structure), the last few toward small-σ (learning *this video's* texture). Limited-interval guidance (Kynkäänniemi et al. NeurIPS 2024) is the inference-time complement: applying full CFG at low σ where it actively degrades visual quality is wasted compute.
- **Cost:** small. ≤ 20-line patch to `compute_flow_matching_loss_conditioned` to accept a `step_idx` and `total_steps` argument and compute the annealing schedule; ≤ 10-line patch to call sites. Limited-interval guidance is a separate 4-line patch to the inference call. No new sbatch wrappers needed; new flags on existing runners.
- **Expected effect:** Probably small effect on PSNR, possibly larger effect on LPIPS / VBench dimensions where global-vs-detail balance matters (Aes, Subj, IQ). Most likely on the long-horizon regime where the §2.3 cohort already shows method-asymmetry (REVIEW §4 — Subj diverges 0.018 between AdaSteer and LoRA r=8 at 76-frame vs 0.005 at 28-frame). Lowest risk of all the modifications.
- **Citation (primary):** Yi et al., "Diffusion Time-step Curriculum for One Image to 3D Generation", CVPR 2024, [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Yi_Diffusion_Time-step_Curriculum_for_One_Image_to_3D_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04562v3); secondary: Kynkäänniemi et al., "Applying Guidance in a Limited Interval Improves Sample and Distribution Quality in Diffusion Models", NeurIPS 2024, [PDF](https://users.aalto.fi/~laines9/publications/kynkaanniemi2024guidance_paper.pdf).
- **Falsification:** held-out population-level Δ on Panda 1000v under `ADA + timestep-curriculum` does *not* exceed Δ under `ADA` baseline by ≥ 0.03 PSNR or ≥ 0.005 LPIPS, *and* the per-VBench-dim shift under curriculum is < 0.005 across all 7 dimensions.
- **Priority: 4.** Easy add — small enough to bundle with Modification 1 in a single sbatch wave if cluster budget permits.

### Modification 5: Continual Streaming TTA with Stochastic Restoration

- **Statement:** Do not reset the adapter state between videos. Initialise the adapter for video `n` from the adapter state after TTA on video `n−1`, with CoTTA-style stochastic weight restoration: at each TTA step, with probability `p_restore`, restore each adapter parameter to its source-initialised value.
- **Mechanism:** Wang et al.'s **Online TTT for Video Streams** (JMLR 2025) reports >2× and 1.5× gains on segmentation by simply not resetting parameters between frames — the **locality** finding is that recent test points carry useful adaptation signal even when video frames are technically i.i.d.-shuffled. CoTTA's stochastic restoration prevents catastrophic forgetting on long non-stationary streams. The hypothesis in our setting: per-video TTA throws away the adaptation signal from the previous 998 videos and starts cold each time. Even if videos are topically uncorrelated (Panda 1000v is hash-ordered), the *low-level statistics* (LongCat-Video's domain gap to Panda-70M's specific encoding artefacts, the VAE's per-codec biases) are video-set-wide and accumulating those across the stream is exactly what implicit memory captures. The stochastic restoration controls drift.
- **Cost:** medium. Requires modifying the per-video loop in all three runners (`run_delta_a.py`, `run_lora_tta.py`, `run_tinylora.py`) to not reset adapter state and to apply stochastic restoration. Importantly, this also requires re-thinking the "chunk_0..chunk_9" structure: the chunks are currently parallelised, so a true streaming run requires either serialising the chunks (10× the wallclock) or running it as a single-chunk N=999 run (also serial). Cost classification: medium-large in cluster cost (~5× current wallclock per full sweep at p_restore=0.01) but small in code change.
- **Expected effect:** Highly uncertain. The literature strongly supports it for segmentation, but classification/regression on i.i.d.-shuffled data is a different setting. Plausible outcomes: (a) large positive effect (Online-TTT-style locality bonus), (b) small positive effect with significant variance reduction (the implicit memory captures the dataset-wide adaptation signal that per-video adaptation misses), (c) near-zero or negative effect (Panda's hash-shuffling means adjacent videos provide adversarial gradient noise rather than locality bonus). The variance-reduction outcome (b) would be very valuable for the paper narrative even if the mean is unchanged.
- **Citation (primary):** Wang, Sun, Gandelsman et al., "Test-Time Training on Video Streams", JMLR 2025, [PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume26/24-0439/24-0439.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05014v1), [project](https://video-ttt.github.io/); secondary: Wang et al., "CoTTA: Continual Test-Time Domain Adaptation", CVPR 2022, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13591).
- **Falsification:** under `LORA_R8_TTA streaming + p_restore=0.01`, the per-video ΔPSNR distribution at the *back* of the stream (videos 800–999) is statistically indistinguishable from the *front* of the stream (videos 0–199) AND from the per-video-reset baseline. This would specifically rule out the locality hypothesis; if it falsifies, the stream-position has no effect and the adapter is not accumulating useful state.
- **Priority: 5.** Most expensive modification by cluster cost and most uncertain by expected effect. Implement last, conditional on Modifications 1 + 2 producing signal worth investing further compute in.

## 5. What the literature does NOT support

- **Theme 6 (test-time prompt ensembling) is dead for our setting.** The NOPROMPT ablation already proved the TTA-time caption is a noise channel (REVIEW §2.2: |ρ(Δ, caption_words)| ≈ −0.02 for NOPROMPT variants); compounding the noise with BLIP-generated alternatives helps zero-shot classification by 5 % but cannot help a video continuation model whose TTA loss does not optimise text alignment. Skip.
- **More TTA iterations is not a recipe modification worth testing.** TTOM (2026) explicitly observes saturation-then-degradation when test-time optimization iterations exceed a soft optimum; this matches the qualitative shape of our saturation. Pushing `--tta-steps` from 10/20 to 50/100 has a literature-backed expectation of *worse* outcomes.
- **End-to-end DiT + VAE adaptation under standard diffusion loss is contraindicated.** REPA-E (ICCV 2025) shows that vanilla diffusion-loss-into-VAE *degrades* performance unless the gradient is mediated by a representation-alignment loss. The naive "just unfreeze everything" recipe is a literature-documented anti-pattern. Modification 2 (VAE-decoder-only with reconstruction loss, DiT frozen) is the supported version.
- **Vanilla MAML-style meta-learned initialisation is impractical at our scale.** The MAML-on-diffusion line (MetaDiff AAAI 2024 and successors) requires backpropagation through the inner-loop optimisation, which on a 700 M-parameter video DiT with 10-step TTA is memory-prohibitive on H200. The hypernetwork variant (HyperDreamBooth-style; Modification 8 in expanded form, deferred above) is the practical path if we choose to invest in amortised TTA — but the training data cost (~130 GPU-h of per-subject ground-truth LoRAs) is itself substantial.

## 7. Open questions for the user

1. **Anchor-loss weight λ.** Literature reports values from 0.01 (FlowLoss) to 10 (typical perceptual/reconstruction-loss balancing in image diffusion fine-tuning), with MiVID reporting an effective sweep around `λ_recon ≈ 1.0` and `λ_LPIPS ≈ 0.1` for video frame interpolation. The right value for *per-video* TTA at our σ-weighting is unknown. **Question:** sweep `λ_anchor ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0}` (4 values × 4 methods = 16 chunked sweeps) or pick a default and report just one? The compute cost of the sweep is ~4× a single-arm sweep on Panda 1000v.
2. **Pure-latent vs VAE-decoded anchor loss.** The pure-latent variant `‖pred_x0 − train_latents‖²` is free (no extra VAE forward); the VAE-decoded variant `‖VAE.decode(pred_x0) − pixel_frames‖²` adds 50–150 ms / step and is more aligned with the eval metric (PSNR is pixel-space). **Question:** start with both as Phase-5 arms, or start with pure-latent only and add the perceptual variant only if pure-latent shows promise?
3. **VAE-decoder-only TTA: replace existing methods or add as a 5th arm?** Modification 2 is a recipe pivot that produces a *different kind* of adapted model — there's no LoRA / TinyLoRA / AdaSteer analogue. The natural comparison is `NOTTA + per-video VAE` vs `NOTTA`, but it could also be `ADA + per-video VAE` (combining DiT adaptation with VAE adaptation). **Question:** which baselines to position against? The full 2×3 (DiT-method × VAE-static-or-tuned) is 6 sbatch arms.
4. **Continual streaming: serial across chunks (true streaming) or per-chunk streaming with reset between chunks (10× parallelism preserved)?** The pure online-TTT formulation requires a serial single-stream, which collapses our 10-way chunked parallelism. Per-chunk streaming (within-chunk implicit memory but between-chunk reset) preserves parallelism at the cost of ~10× fewer videos per adaptation cycle. **Question:** worth the wallclock hit for the cleanest experimental design, or accept the within-chunk version?
5. **Anchor-loss + augmentation-consistency-loss interaction.** Modifications 1 and 3 both add auxiliary loss terms to the existing flow-matching MSE. The literature does not strongly support a particular combination weighting (`L = L_flow + λ_anchor · L_anchor + λ_consistency · L_consistency`). **Question:** test Modifications 1 and 3 independently first (the cleaner experimental design), or test the combination directly to avoid burning two sbatch waves if neither standalone helps but the combination does?
